Biomedical Materials & Drug Delivery

Surface Engineering of Titanium Implants and Ultrasound-Responsive Microbubbles

This research explores surface engineering of commercially pure titanium (CP-Ti) stents and ultrasound-mediated drug delivery. Electropolishing is used to develop smooth, clean and corrosion-resistant titanium surfaces, while anodization creates controlled TiO₂ nanotube layers. In parallel, ultrasound-responsive lipid microbubbles are investigated as a platform for localized, on-demand drug delivery and cancer theranostics.

Research focus: electrolyte systems and process parameters; surface bioactivity and wettability; microbubble synthesis, stability and anti-cancer activity.

Biomaterials · Localized Drug Delivery

Multifunctional Injectable Hydrogel for Periodontal Tissue Regeneration

Periodontitis is a chronic inflammatory disease associated with microbial dysbiosis and progressive damage to periodontal tissues and alveolar bone. This project develops a chitosan–β-glycerophosphate thermoresponsive injectable hydrogel for localized, minimally invasive delivery into the periodontal pocket. The platform is designed for dual-action release: an antioxidant and antimicrobial agent for rapid reactive oxygen species clearance and biofilm suppression, alongside polymeric nanospheres for sustained delivery of osteogenic and anti-resorptive therapeutics.

Research aim: coordinate rapid local control of oxidative stress and biofilm with sustained therapeutic delivery to support periodontal tissue and alveolar bone regeneration.
